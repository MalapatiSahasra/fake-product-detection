Research project · IEEE conference paper

Is that product real? Four classical vision checks decide.

A lightweight, training-free system that checks a product photo against a gallery of genuine references using logo keypoints, color, printed text and barcodes, then combines the results with a majority vote. There's no neural network and no GPU, and it takes about 107 ms per image on a CPU.

The problem

Online shoppers judge authenticity from a few photos.

Counterfeits are common in e-commerce, and a buyer often has nothing to go on except the listing images. We wanted to see how far interpretable, classical computer vision can get on that problem without any model training.

01 — NO TRAINING

Works from reference photos

Supply a gallery of genuine images and it's ready. There are no labels to collect and no model to fit.

02 — INTERPRETABLE

Every verdict is explainable

Each check reports its own score, so you can see exactly why a photo passed or failed.

03 — CPU ONLY

Fast on ordinary hardware

About 107 ms per image on a laptop CPU, built on OpenCV, Tesseract and ZBar.

How it works

A cascade of four independent checks

The test photo is compared against a whole gallery of genuine references instead of a single master image, because real catalogs vary too much in angle, model and lighting for one image to stand for "genuine." A photo passes if at most one check disagrees.

01

Logo matcher

ORB keypoints matched with a brute-force Hamming matcher, filtered by Lowe's ratio test.

modules/logo_matcher.py
02

Color inspector

Hue–saturation histogram correlation. The Value channel is dropped so lighting matters less.

modules/color_inspector.py
03

Typo detective

Tesseract reads printed text, and string similarity flags misspelled brand names.

modules/typo_detective.py
04

QR / barcode scanner

Decodes codes with pyzbar and checks that the serial matches the expected format.

modules/qr_scanner.py

KEY FINDING

When it says "genuine," it's usually right.

Precision is high: about 7 in 10 "genuine" verdicts are correct. Recall is limited because real catalogs are very diverse. The ablation also showed that the color check alone (F1 0.650) beats the full 2-of-3 cascade, because the text check rarely finds readable brand text in lifestyle photos. Tuning the vote threshold, or weighting checks differently, is the clearest way to improve results.
